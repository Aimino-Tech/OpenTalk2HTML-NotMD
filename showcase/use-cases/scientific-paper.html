Sparse Multimodal Attention:
Efficient Cross-Modal Understanding with Sublinear Complexity

James Park, Sarah Chen, Michael Torres, Dr. Anna Kowalski
Nova AI Research • Stanford University • MIT CSAIL

Abstract

We present Sparse Multimodal Attention (SMA), a novel attention mechanism for multimodal transformer models that achieves state-of-the-art results on 12 of 14 benchmarks while reducing computational requirements by 60%. SMA employs a learned sparsity mask to selectively attend to relevant cross-modal interactions, replacing the dense O(n²) attention matrix with a sparse O(n log n) alternative. Our method achieves 4.2% accuracy improvement over previous dense attention mechanisms on video understanding tasks and 3.8% on cross-modal retrieval benchmarks.

1. Introduction

Multimodal understanding — the ability to reason across text, images, video, and audio — represents a critical frontier in artificial intelligence. However, existing multimodal architectures face a fundamental scalability challenge: the quadratic complexity of cross-modal attention mechanisms limits their applicability to large-scale, real-world problems.

In this paper, we introduce Sparse Multimodal Attention (SMA), which addresses the computational bottleneck of cross-modal attention by replacing the dense attention matrix with a learned sparse structure.

2. Method

2.1 Sparse Attention Mask

The core innovation of SMA is the learned sparsity mask M ∈ {0,1}^(n×m), where n is the sequence length of the query modality and m is the sequence length of the key modality. Each entry M_ij indicates whether token i in modality A should attend to token j in modality B.

Attention(Q, K, V) = softmax(⊙ QKᵀ / √d) V

where ⊙ denotes element-wise multiplication with the binary mask M. The mask is learned through a Gumbel-Sigmoid relaxation, enabling differentiable training of binary decisions.

2.2 Complexity Analysis

Standard dense cross-modal attention requires O(n·m) operations. SMA reduces this to O(n·k) where k ≪ m is the average number of attended positions per query. In our experiments, k scales as O(log m), yielding near-linear complexity.

3. Results

We evaluated SMA on 14 standard multimodal benchmarks spanning video understanding, cross-modal retrieval, and visual question answering. SMA achieves state-of-the-art results on 12 benchmarks while reducing FLOPs by an average of 60% compared to dense attention baselines.

4. Conclusion

We introduced Sparse Multimodal Attention, a novel approach that achieves state-of-the-art multimodal understanding with dramatically reduced computational requirements. Our work opens new possibilities for deploying large multimodal models in resource-constrained environments.
